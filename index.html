A conceptual heteroclinic decision network. States organize behavior; competing transitions enable a choice.

Featured research / Stable heteroclinic channels

How should a robot
decide what comes next?

I study stable heteroclinic channel (SHC) networks for sequencing and switching robot behaviors. The system moves between neighborhoods of saddle states, making the active state and available transitions explicit.

A central question is how previous activity influences the next decision—and when noise can reduce that influence enough to help a robot respond to a changing input.

Research contributions

  • Constructing networks with prescribed behavior sequences and branching choices.
  • Analyzing transient memory, noise, dwell times, and switching probabilities through dynamical-systems analysis and Monte Carlo simulations.
  • Developing high-level decision networks for integration with flapping-wing control and odor-guided behavior.
Why this structure matters

Explicit states and connections make the controller’s decision structure inspectable. This does not automatically explain every trajectory or make an arbitrary neural network transparent. My work examines how state geometry, inputs, and noise shape the resulting choices.

Current direction: connecting the high-level behavior network to sensing and low-level robot control. The diagram above is a conceptual network, not a measured trajectory or hardware validation.

Soft robotic nasal simulator

An innovative robotic head system combining an anatomically accurate nasal cavity with autonomous breathing and simulating nasal congestion capabilities.

Methods, results & figure notes

Key Innovations Anatomically Precise Design (Fig. 1): Created a segmented head model from real patient CT data, featuring detailed nasal turbinates and airways with 99.5% dimensional accuracy Dynamic Breathing Simulation (Fig. 1): Developed a custom bi-directional fan system with closed-loop PID control to replicate natural breathing patterns Simulating Nasal Cycle Morphology (Video 3): Developed a pipelined algorithm, combining active contour and path planning algorithms to predict intermediate nasal morphology

Physical Dynamic Nasal Morphology (Video 1): Integrated pneumatically driven actuators inside the nasal cavity to simulate nasal congestions and nasal cycle behaviors Numerical Modeling of Nasal Cycle (Video 2): Nasal wall membrane displacement is numerically analyzed with ABAQUS against the congestion level to obtain the operating pressure range for embedded pneumatic actuators

02

Benchtop lab-on-chip incubator

Developed a cost-effective, compact benchtop cell-culture incubator optimized for nose-on-chip devices which could accommodate airflow systems required for respiratory tissue modeling.

Methods, results & figure notes

Key Innovations Created a modular design with customizable ports for simulating breathing airflow for LOC devices. Developed a control system with PIDs to control constant temperature with a precision of ± 0.3°C while minimizing the temperature gradient across the incubator. Designed the electronic circuit to regulate the voltage supplied to the heating elements based on the thermal sensor readings and soldered the electronic components into circuit boards. Achieved stable temperature maintenance and rapid recovery with PD controllers

Robotic peristaltic conduit

An innovative bio-inspired worm-like robotic system is developed for underground power line installation. This project demonstrates the versatility of soft robotics in addressing complex locomotion challenges in confined and difficult-to-access spaces.

Methods, results & figure notes

Key Innovations Cable Driven Design (Figure 1) A Robot driven by motors and cables was developed to move the power line conduit underground Robust Pneumatic System (Figure 2 and Video 1) Converted the driving method of the robot to pneumatics to minimize the complexity associated with cables by designing a pneumatic circuit to deliver consistent pressure to all the expanding segments (100 ft) with minimum pressure drop.

Fig. 1 - Initial prototype of the robot which has been developed by us to drag the power line conduit underground. This robot is actuated by electrical motors with tendon driven mechanism.

Video 1 - Testing of pneumatic circuit to operate the robot with peristaltic locomotion

Fig. 2 - Testing Platform designed to test pneumatic circuits in which can deliver consistent pressure for all inflatable actuators

07

Analytical modeling of layer jamming

A novel analytical model for predicting and optimizing layer jamming mechanism performance in soft robotics applications by providing a practical, experimentally validated model for designing variable stiffness systems.

Methods, results & figure notes

Key Innovations Created a multi-parameter analytical model accounting for different materials and layer combinations Successfully validated model across multiple material combinations and operating condition, achieving R-squared values ranging from 0.801 to 0.945 across different material combinations Material Integration: Successfully modeled complex interactions between different material types and layer arrangements

08

Soft robotic glove for tremor suppression

Developed an innovative, lightweight soft robotic glove using layer jamming technology to suppress hand tremors, providing a non-invasive, comfortable solution that achieved significant tremor reduction in clinical trials.

Methods, results & figure notes

Key Innovations First implementation of layer jamming technology for tremor suppression Clinical Validation: Achieved 41.74% - 41.99% tremor reduction in finger movements and 24.71% in grasping tasks Ultra-lightweight (30g) with minimal impact on voluntary movement Optimized Performance: Systematic testing of multiple material combinations and configurations Material Integration: Successfully modeled complex interactions between different material types and layer arrangements

Prototype glove with layer jamming elements used in clinical trials

Presenting at RoboSoft 2020 Conference Proceedings
